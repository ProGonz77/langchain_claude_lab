1. COURSE DESCRIPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------
This course introduces the foundational ideas, techniques, and
applications of Artificial Intelligence (AI). Students will study
how machines can search, reason, represent knowledge, handle
uncertainty, and learn from data. The course covers both classical
AI (search, logic, planning) and modern approaches (machine
learning, neural networks, and generative models), with attention
to the ethical and societal impact of AI systems.


--------------------------------------------------------------
2. LEARNING OBJECTIVES
--------------------------------------------------------------
By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Explain the history, goals, and major subfields of AI.
  2. Implement and analyze uninformed and informed search
     algorithms, including A*.
  3. Formulate problems as constraint satisfaction problems and
     adversarial games.
  4. Represent knowledge using propositional and first-order logic.
  5. Apply probabilistic reasoning, including Bayesian networks
     and Markov models.
  6. Build and evaluate supervised and unsupervised machine
     learning models.
  7. Describe how neural networks and large language models work.
  8. Discuss the ethical, legal, and social implications of AI,
     including bias, privacy, safety, and accountability.

4. WEEKLY SCHEDULE
--------------------------------------------------------------
Week 1   Introduction to AI
         History, definitions, intelligent agents, environments.
         Reading: Chapters 1-2

Week 2   Uninformed Search
         BFS, DFS, uniform-cost search, iterative deepening.
         Reading: Chapter 3

Week 3   Informed Search
         Heuristics, greedy best-first search, A*, admissibility.
         Reading: Chapter 3
         ** Assignment 1 due: Search Algorithms (Pathfinding) **

Week 4   Local Search and Optimization
         Hill climbing, simulated annealing, genetic algorithms.
         Reading: Chapter 4

Week 5   Adversarial Search and Games
         Minimax, alpha-beta pruning, Monte Carlo tree search.
         Reading: Chapter 5

Week 6   Constraint Satisfaction Problems
         Backtracking, arc consistency, constraint propagation.
         Reading: Chapter 6
         ** Assignment 2 due: Game-Playing Agent **

Week 7   Logical Agents
         Propositional logic, inference, resolution, SAT.
         Reading: Chapters 7-8

Week 8   MIDTERM EXAM (Weeks 1-7)
         First-order logic and knowledge representation.

Week 9   Probabilistic Reasoning
         Bayes' rule, Bayesian networks, inference methods.
         Reading: Chapters 12-13

Week 10  Reasoning Over Time
         Hidden Markov models, Kalman filters, decision-making
         under uncertainty, Markov decision processes.
         Reading: Chapters 14, 16-17
         ** Assignment 3 due: Bayesian Network Inference **

Week 11  Machine Learning Fundamentals
         Supervised learning, decision trees, regression,
         overfitting, cross-validation.
         Reading: Chapter 19

Week 12  Neural Networks and Deep Learning
         Perceptrons, backpropagation, CNNs, optimization.
         Reading: Chapter 21

Week 13  Reinforcement Learning
         Q-learning, policy gradients, exploration vs.
         exploitation.
         Reading: Chapter 22
         ** Assignment 4 due: Neural Network Classifier **

Week 14  Natural Language Processing and Generative AI
         Transformers, large language models, prompting,
         limitations such as hallucination.
         Reading: Chapters 23-24

Week 15  AI Ethics, Safety, and Society
         Fairness, bias, privacy, explainability, regulation,
         and the future of work.
         Reading: Chapter 27
         ** Final Project presentations **

Finals   FINAL EXAM (Comprehensive) - date set by Registrar

6. COURSE POLICIES
--------------------------------------------------------------
Late Work:
  Assignments lose 10% per day late, up to 3 days. After that,
  no credit is given unless prior arrangements were made.

Attendance:
  Regular attendance is expected. Students are responsible for
  all material and announcements covered in class.

Academic Integrity:
  All submitted work must be your own. Collaboration is allowed
  only on the final project and where explicitly stated.
  Using AI tools (e.g., code assistants or chatbots) is allowed
  only for brainstorming and debugging, and must be disclosed in
  a short note with each submission. Copying solutions, from
  AI tools or other people, without understanding or attribution
  is a violation of the university honor code and may result in
  failure of the course.

Accessibility and Accommodations:
  Students who need accommodations should contact the Office of
  Disability Services and notify the instructor within the first
  two weeks of the semester.

Changes to Syllabus:
  The instructor may adjust the schedule as needed. Any changes
  will be announced in class and posted online.


--------------------------------------------------------------
7. FINAL PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------
Teams will design, build, and evaluate an AI system addressing a
real-world problem of their choice (e.g., a game agent, image
classifier, recommendation system, or chatbot). Deliverables:

  - Proposal (due Week 6)
  - Progress report (due Week 11)
  - Final presentation and 6-8 page written report (Week 15)
  - Well-documented code repository

Projects are evaluated on technical soundness, creativity,
clarity of communication, and discussion of ethical impact.
